The Problem

Most routine apps rely on rigid schedules and streaks, but real life is unpredictable—when plans break, users struggle to recover. This can lead to guilt, overwhelming catch-up lists, and eventually abandoned routines.

Research

I explored how people build and maintain routines, especially what happens when plans are interrupted.
I looked at:

  • How users plan and structure routines
  • What happens when tasks are missed
  • How streaks and missed days affect motivation
  • How energy levels influence completion
  • What helps users restart after falling off track

Key Insight

People don’t fail routines because they’re lazy—they fail when routines are too rigid for real life. Momentum shifts the focus from perfect streaks to continuity, recovery, and getting back on track.

Solution

Momentum is designed around one question: What’s realistically achievable today?

  • Time Blocks — Organize routines by Morning, Midday, Evening, and Before Sleep
  • Adaptive Compression — Shorten and reprioritize routines when time is limited
  • Energy-Based Planning — Adjust activities based on Low, Medium, or High energy
  • Momentum Score — Reward consistency, small wins, and recovery instead of streaks
  • Shame-Free Restart — Help users continue after missed days without focusing on what they missed
  • Overflow System — Let users Move, Simplify, or Skip tasks instead of creating a backlog

Project Outcome

What I Learned

Designing Momentum taught me that productivity isn’t about forcing consistency—it’s about supporting people when consistency breaks.

My biggest takeaway: Good routine design isn’t about creating a perfect schedule; it’s about creating a system people can confidently return to.

This project strengthened my understanding of behavioral UX, adaptive systems, emotional design, and designing for sustainable habits.

The Problem

Choosing a college or studying abroad is a major decision, yet many education consultancy websites feel crowded, outdated, and overly promotional.

EduHope needed a modern, trustworthy website that clearly communicates its services while making students and parents feel confident about reaching out.

The design challenge:
How might we make EduHope feel credible, approachable, and different from other education consultancies?

Research

I reviewed EduHope’s services, target audience, and competitor websites to understand what students look for when choosing an education consultancy.

  • Students need clear information and guidance
  • Trust and credibility are important before making an enquiry
  • There was an opportunity to make EduHope feel clearer, more modern, and approachable

Solution

I designed EduHope around one simple idea:

Make the journey from discovering EduHope to making an enquiry feel effortless.

  • Clear Services — Organised key services so visitors can quickly understand how EduHope can help
  • Strong Visual Hierarchy — Used clear typography, spacing, and sections to make information easy to scan.
  • Trust-Focused Design — Highlighted credibility and reasons to choose EduHope before asking users to enquire.
  • Modern Visual Identity — Used minimal layouts, soft imagery, subtle gradients, and generous whitespace.
  • Clear Conversion Path — Guided users through Explore → Understand → Trust → Contact
  • Responsive Design — Ensured the experience remains clear and usable across different screen sizes.

Project Outcome

Service Clarity
Easy-to-understand services
Build Trust
Credibility before enquiry
Easy Enquiry
Simple route to contact

What I Learned

The project showed me that a successful client website isn't just about looking better — it needs to solve a real communication problem for the business.

By improving the structure, visual identity, and enquiry journey, I helped EduHope move from a basic website requirement to a clearer and more professional digital presence.
